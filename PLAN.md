# Plan

The decisions, and why. Everything follows the friend's design (BRIEF.md); where the design was silent, this says what was chosen.

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Built on Long Time Comin's engine: Q16.16 core, instanced WebGL 2 draws, ink outlines, toon bands, fixed 60 ticks. | The house 3D engine, tested in three shipped games. |
| P2 | Ground is a heightfield (2 m grid) built from data shapes; drawn as a per-hole mesh with a tint per vertex (a new vertex attribute). | Collision and drawing share one surface; holes are written as hills, plateaus and ridges, not by hand. |
| P3 | The meter: a needle round a dial, clockwise from twelve, 1.3 s a lap. The red bar runs 105°–255°, and the gold 7° either side of its middle is perfect. Power falls to 72% at the bar's ends; early hooks, late slices, off the bar is a whiff at 30%. | "A circle that spins around and has a power bar with perfect shot in the middle." Distance is set before the swing with a power setting (the mallet's weight), so the meter judges the hit and does not choose the distance. |
| P4 | The catapult is a plank on a fulcrum with a striker pad. A mallet on a post swings down from the side, with a striped striker tower and bell beside it. The bell rings on a perfect hit. | "Similar to the ring the bell strength test games at carnivals." The mallet swings in from the side so the view behind the frog stays clear. |
| P5 | Air control: W A S D accelerate the frog sideways and along (7 m/s²), relative to the camera. | "Players take control of their frog as it flies." |
| P6 | Extra jump: Space in the air, carried up to three. Wings: a 6.5 s glide in which A and D bank, W dives and S climbs, trading speed for height. Mushroom: on touch, 24 m/s up and 15 m/s away. | The design's three power-ups. Power-ups come back every stroke, so each player gets the same course. |
| P7 | Flies are counted per frog across the round. Each frog can catch each fly once per hole. The tongue costs 10 flies, reaches 11 m, and works while aiming or flying. | "Like rings from Sonic", "collecting 10 flies allows… E… knockback". |
| P8 | Fly lines: the first line on each hole is placed along the arc of an imagined real shot, and the rest are straight or arched lines. | Flies at a fixed height under a 20 m-high arc were never caught (SECOND-ORDER S3). |
| P9 | Turn order as in golf: everyone tees off in order, then the frog farthest from the lotus plays. Hazards cost one stroke, with stroke and distance. A hole is given up at par + 5. | "Players all start at the same driving tee just like a game of regular golf." |
| P10 | Four holes, one per theme the design names: swamp (par 3), desert oasis (4), busy city street with traffic (4), volcano lava flow (5). | The design's four themes. The traffic is what makes the street busy. |
| P11 | Eight frogs in the roster: red-eyed tree frog, blue and yellow-banded dart frogs, tomato frog, cane toad, golden toad, horned frog, glass frog. | "Different colours and patterns like tree frogs and toads… large eyes." |
| P12 | CPU players: imagine shots over five lines × three lofts × nineteen powers; re-test the best eight against a weak or crooked hit; miss the meter by up to 22°. | So one person can play a full round. |
| P13 | The leader after each hole wears a crown on the course; the final card is headed "The Ribbit King". | The title, made into the prize. |
| P14 | No pointer lock; the mouse drags the camera. Touch buttons appear on coarse pointers. | A turn-based golf game needs the cursor for its screens. |
| P15 | Records (best per hole, best full round, rounds played) are kept in local storage, for human players only. | Allowed by Builder; a project decision. |
| P16 | Online play: up to four browsers, using Floodline's and Vagrancy's transport (`web/net/rtc.js`: WebRTC, a star, by room code over Trystero and Nostr relays, or by pasted code). On top of it runs a host's tick stream: the host runs the round and sends each tick's input (the playing seat's keys plus a command) to every joiner, and each joiner runs the same round from the same steps. | Sam asked for "4 player online multiplayer using other multiplayer frameworks from builder and sgilson7 repos" (BRIEF, round 2). Floodline and Vagrancy wait on every peer's input every tick, but golf is one frog at a time, so here only the playing seat's input matters. Nobody waits on the other three, and the host decides whose input counts. |
| P17 | A whack online is judged by the needle as the swinging player saw it (`Press::needle`). | At 1.3 s a lap, a 100 ms round trip moves the needle 28°, and the perfect zone is 14° wide. |
| P18 | A player who leaves mid-round is played by the CPU from the next tick, on every copy; the host leaving ends the round for everyone. A joiner joins only before a round starts. | The round keeps its four frogs. There is no host migration, which Floodline also does not have. |
| P19 | Online, nothing pauses: Esc draws the menu over a round that goes on. | One person's pause would freeze three others. |
| P20 | The relays are pinned in `web/net/config.js` rather than Trystero's defaults. | The default list included a relay that redirected every handshake (SECOND-ORDER S14). |
| P21 | Five holes, all at least par 5 (volcano par 6), 270 m wide and about 400 m long: Lily Swamp, Oasis Dunes, **Toadstool Hollow** (new, the fungus course), Main Street, Lava Flow. They are generated by `tools/courses.py` into `data/course.json` from one plan. | Round 3. A generator keeps five large holes consistent; every number still lives in the data file the core reads. |
| P22 | Three routes on every hole: A starts left and B starts right, and they cross each other on a 8–10 m terrace across the middle of the hole. C runs straight down the middle and passes under that terrace through a tunnel, so the routes cross over one another. | "three distinct routes… cross over one another". |
| P23 | New ground features, all carved into the heightfield by the core: mesas and terraces with cliff edges, river and pond beds, tunnel cuts, web pits and antlion funnels. Rivers drop over waterfalls between reaches at different levels. | Plateaus, hills, cliffs, tunnels, rivers and waterfalls, as asked. Each feature is one line of data. |
| P24 | Water costs nothing: the frog swims where it lands and plays its next shot from a lily pad there. Lava and out of bounds still cost a stroke. Each lotus floats in a pond, so a near miss is a chip shot from the water. | "Frogs are able to swim in water hazards and it does not count as an extra stroke." |
| P25 | Webs are set flush into the ground over pits, 3–9 m in radius, and a bigger web throws harder (19 m/s at 3 m, +1.6 m/s a metre). On a bounce the web sinks into a deep funnel, springs back past flat and settles: after the web over the pit in the Deku Tree. | As asked. |
| P26 | Giant blue mushrooms are pinball bumpers: the cap throws a frog off at no less than 22 m/s, and the cap squashes and lights up when it does. The small red explosive mushrooms stay as they were. | As asked. |
| P27 | Point bubbles of 10–100 points, along the routes, in the rough, in the tunnel mouth and above the crossing. Points are kept per hole and in total, apart from strokes, and shown on the HUD, the standings and the scorecard. | As asked. |
| P28 | Each fly caught is one small hop (3.8 m/s up) at the next landing. | "One tiny bounce per fly." |
| P29 | A perfect hit fires a rocket from the top of the striker tower's bell, and its shell bursts a moment later. | As asked. |
| P30 | The crown goes to whoever is in first place now: fewest strokes over the holes played and this one so far. Nobody wears it while two or more are level. | "The frog currently in first place." |
| P31 | Swamp: snails crawl to and fro across the routes and their shells block bounces. Whirlpools in the river and the pond spin a frog for 1.5 s and throw it out along the way it was turning. Desert: antlion pits grab a frog that lands near the bottom; its turn ends there, and its next shot is from the pit. | Sam's second message. |
| P32 | Power-ups sit in the rough by a seeded roll, away from the routes, and one sits beside each route's fairway. | "Around the fairway and in the rough." |
| P33 | The CPU searches a few imagined shots a tick (6) instead of all at once. It tries lines at the lotus, toward fairway ends ahead and the eight compass points. | A single search on the big holes took 0.2–0.46 s and would have stalled the page. The extra lines let the CPU follow routes and get out from against walls. |
